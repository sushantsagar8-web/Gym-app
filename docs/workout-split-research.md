# FitPath Workout Split and Cardio Research

Research captured on 2026-08-17 for Shop program design. This document is a design reference, not medical advice; exercise prescriptions should be adapted to experience, equipment, and limitations.

## Workout split findings

NASM describes the common five-day bro split as Chest, Back, Legs, Shoulders, and Arms, with each major muscle group receiving a focused day. It notes that bro splits can work when weekly volume, progressive overload, and recovery are managed, but there is no magical superiority to the split itself. Source: [NASM, “Bro Splits: Do They Actually Work?”](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/bro-splits)

Aston University describes Push/Pull/Legs as Push for chest, shoulders, and triceps; Pull for back and biceps; and Legs for quads, hamstrings, calves, and abdominals. It presents a rotating structure that can be used across three to six sessions depending on schedule. Source: [Aston University, “The Push/Pull/Legs Routine for Muscle Gains”](https://www.aston.ac.uk/sport/news/tips/fitness-exercise/push-pull-legs)

YouTube was reviewed for practical split demonstrations, including [Jeremy Ethier, “I Tested EVERY Workout Split (Using Science)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GRZhpHKZqOA), [Jeremy Ethier, “The Smartest Push Pull Legs Routine”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qVek72z3F1U), and [Seth Feroce, “Bro Split vs Push Pull Legs Explained”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cI3VBiUSttM). These videos were used as practical programming references only; the app’s structure follows the clearer muscle-group definitions above rather than copying creator-specific routines.

## Cardio findings

The CDC recommends adults accumulate at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity each week, plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days. It also gives brisk walking and jogging/running as examples. Source: [CDC, “Adult Activity: An Overview”](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)

ACSM describes HIIT as short high-intensity bouts alternating with brief lower-intensity recovery periods. It also cautions that intensity should progress gradually and that adherence and injury risk matter when selecting a regimen. Source: [ACSM, “High-Intensity Interval Training: For Fitness, for Health or Both?”](https://acsm.org/high-intensity-interval-training-fitness/)

Cleveland Clinic identifies swimming, cycling, rowing, elliptical training, vertical climbing, walking, and hiking as lower-impact cardio choices that can raise heart rate while reducing joint impact compared with running. Source: [Cleveland Clinic, “7 Low-Impact Cardio Exercises To Try”](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/low-impact-cardio)

## FitPath design translation

The Shop catalog now separates the men’s abs foundation, women’s abs foundation, general fitness foundation, muscle-building bro split, muscle-building Push & Pull Days, and a cardio foundation. The cardio program intentionally uses content placeholders for brisk walking, cycling, rowing, and low-impact bodyweight cardio until the user supplies licensed exercise videos.

## Bro Split refinement research — 2026-08-17

The implementation uses a four-session weekly structure requested by the product owner: Monday Chest & Triceps, Tuesday Back & Biceps, Wednesday Shoulders & Abs, and Thursday Legs & Abs, followed by recovery days. This is a product-specific adaptation rather than a claim that one split is universally optimal.

NASM describes the most common traditional Bro Split as five separate workouts for chest, back, legs, shoulders, and arms, and also gives a three-day paired variant of chest/shoulders/triceps, back/biceps, and legs. Its example exercises include presses and flys for chest, rows and pulldowns for back, curls for biceps, extensions and dips for triceps, squats/hinges/curls/calf work for legs, and presses/raises/shrugs for shoulders. Source: https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/bro-splits

Muscle & Strength’s 10 Week Mass Building Program is a four-day example with Monday Chest & Triceps, Tuesday Back & Biceps, Thursday Shoulders & Forearms, and Friday Legs. Its exercise examples include bench and incline pressing, flys, triceps extensions and dips; rows, pulldowns, and curls; shoulder presses, reverse flys, lateral raises, and shrugs; and squats, leg extensions, leg curls, and calf raises. Source PDF: https://www.muscleandstrength.com/sites/default/files/workouts/10weekmass.pdf

YouTube references reviewed for split comparisons and practical explanations include Jeremy Ethier’s “I Tested EVERY Workout Split (Using Science)” at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GRZhpHKZqOA and “Every Training Split Explained In 10 Minutes” at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f8taCG6VWbE. These were used as contextual references for explaining split choices, not as a substitute for individualized medical or coaching advice.
